Privacy Policy

Last updated: 5 August 2026

Draw-tionary is a drawing game for Discord. This page describes exactly what it stores, why it needs it, how long it keeps it, and how to have it removed. It was written against the actual database schema rather than from a template.

The short version. Draw-tionary stores your Discord user ID, the drawings you make, the guesses you submit, and your score. It never sees your email, your real name, your message history, or anything you type in Discord outside of a guess you deliberately submit. Nothing is sold, and nothing is shared with advertisers.

Who runs this

Draw-tionary is operated by an individual, not a company. It's open source — you can read the code that does everything described here at github.com/tracyapps/draw-tionary.

For questions, bugs, or anything you'd be happy discussing in public: open an issue on GitHub.

For a data request — deleting your data, or asking what's stored about you: email privacy@draw-tionary.app. Please don't use a public GitHub issue for this: it would require posting your Discord user ID where anyone can read it, which is the opposite of what a deletion request is for.

What is stored

Identifiers from Discord

DataWhy it's needed
Your Discord user ID To know whose drawing is whose, stop you guessing your own word, and keep your score. This is the numeric ID, not your username.
Server (guild) ID Scores and drawings are kept per server, so one community's game never mixes with another's.
Channel ID and message ID So the bot can post a drawing in the right place and find the round again when someone presses a button on it.

Draw-tionary does not receive or store your email address, your real name, your avatar, your IP address, or your Discord password. It has no access to your message history — it holds no live connection to your server and cannot read messages at all.

Game content you create

DataWhy it's needed
Your drawings, as stroke data Every line, colour, and the timing between them, so the drawing can be replayed stroke by stroke. This is the game.
The word you were dealt To score guesses against it.
Every guess you submit, including wrong ones To stop double-scoring, and to count your attempts. Wrong guesses are shown only to you, never to other players.
Your score per server For the /scores leaderboard.
Reports you submit on a drawing So two reports can hide a drawing for review. Reports are visible only to moderators — the drawer never learns who reported them.
Moderator actions Who removed or restored a drawing, and when, so there is a record that a review happened.

Session data

When you open a drawing canvas or a replay, Draw-tionary creates a short-lived record linking your browser to your Discord account:

  • Drawing sessions — a single-use link that expires after 30 minutes.
  • Replay grants — a single-use link that expires after 90 seconds and is exchanged immediately for a cookie.
  • A viewer cookie (dt_viewer) — set when you open a replay, valid for 7 days. It holds a random token and nothing else. It is HttpOnly, so page scripts cannot read it.

Cookies

Draw-tionary sets exactly one cookie, dt_viewer, described above. It exists so a replay page knows whether you already solved that drawing and can show you the answer you earned.

There are no analytics cookies, no advertising cookies, and no third-party trackers on any page. Nothing follows you off the site.

How long it's kept

DataRetention
Drawings, guesses, and scores Kept until deleted. Drawings deliberately do not expire — the point is that you can go back and find the thing you spent an hour on.
Removed drawings The record that a removal happened is kept for moderation history. The artwork itself is erased after a cooling-off period.
Drawing sessions and replay grants Deleted automatically once expired; swept hourly.
Viewer cookie 7 days, or until you clear your browser's cookies.

Who it's shared with

Nobody. Draw-tionary does not sell data, does not share it with advertisers, and has no analytics or tracking provider.

Two unavoidable parties are involved in running it:

  • Discord — the game lives inside Discord, so your drawings and scores are posted there. Discord's own privacy policy covers that.
  • Railway — the hosting provider where the server and its database run.

Your choices

  • Delete a drawing. Every drawing you make has a delete option. You don't need to ask anyone.
  • Delete everything. Email privacy@draw-tionary.app with your Discord user ID, and your drawings, guesses and scores will be erased.
  • Get a copy of your data. Ask at the same address and you'll receive what's stored about you in a readable format.
  • Stop entirely. Remove the bot from your server, or simply stop using /draw. Nothing is collected when you aren't playing.

Requests are handled within 30 days. Depending on where you live you may have additional rights under laws such as the GDPR or CCPA — including access, correction, deletion, and complaint to a regulator. The steps above are how to exercise them.

Children

Discord requires users to be at least 13, or older where local law sets a higher age. Draw-tionary is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly store their data. If you believe a child under 13 has used it, contact the address above and their data will be removed.

Security

All traffic is served over HTTPS. Links to a canvas or a replay are single-use and short-lived, and the cookie that replaces them cannot be read by page scripts. The word being guessed is never sent to a player who hasn't earned it.

No system is perfectly secure, and this one is run by one person rather than a security team. Please don't put anything sensitive in a drawing.

Changes

If this policy changes materially, the date at the top will be updated and a note will be posted on the project repository. Because the project is open source, every change to this page is also visible in the commit history.

Note. This policy was written to describe what the software genuinely does, and is not legal advice. If Draw-tionary grows beyond a hobby project, have a lawyer review it — particularly the GDPR and children's-privacy sections, which depend on where your players actually live.
